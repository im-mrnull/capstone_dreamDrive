Abstract and Scope
●

Current autonomous driving systems rely primarily on reactive
decision-making, predicting a single, most-likely future path for surrounding
agents.

●

This approach fails in chaotic, unstructured traffic environments such as
those found in India, where pedestrians, rickshaws, and two-wheelers behave
unpredictably.

●

There is therefore a pressing need for a proactive prediction model that does
not depend on a single deterministic forecast, but instead simulates multiple
possible futures.

●

DreamDrive addresses this challenge by enabling autonomous vehicles to
anticipate risks before they occur, ensuring safer navigation in mixed-traffic
environments.

Design Constraints
●

Dataset Constraints:
Limited availability of large-scale, well-annotated Indian chaotic traffic datasets;
significant behavioral and structural domain gap between Indian and Western
datasets.

●

Computational Constraints:
1. Multi-future prediction (K trajectories per agent) increases inference time
and memory requirements, limiting real-time scalability.
2. Best-of-K training further increases training cost due to oracle selection.

●

Simulation Constraints:
1. CARLA does not natively model all Indian traffic behaviors (lane-less driving,
informal overtaking, rickshaw dynamics), requiring approximations.
2. Traffic density and heterogeneity must be artificially scripted in CARLA to
approximate Indian scenarios.

Assumptions
●

Traffic agents exhibit locally consistent motion patterns over short
prediction horizons.

●

Past trajectory history and local spatial context are sufficient to infer
near-future behavior.

●

Agent detection and trajectory annotations are assumed to be reasonably
accurate.

●

Road geometry and environmental context remain static during the
prediction window.

Size and Type of Data
● Data Type: 3D Object Detection and Trajectory data. The raw input consists of JSON label
files per frame, containing oriented bounding box (OBB) information ($psr.position.x, y, z$).
● Scale (Processed):
○ Single-Agent Windows: N = 4,821 trajectory windows.
○ Multi-Agent Scenes: Exported as 138 validation scenes (based on the 80/20 split
mentioned in the Trajectron++ section).
● Format: The pipeline converts raw JSONs into structured CSV (positions), NPZ (numpy
binaries for training), and Pickle (for Trajectron++) formats.

○ Prediction Horizon: 60 frames (6.0 seconds).
○ Stride: 3 frames.

Architecture
Data Preparation and Trajectory Construction:
● Raw traffic datasets are parsed to extract precise agent position information, which is then
segmented into fixed-length trajectory windows suitable for temporal modeling.
Model Training and Prediction:
● Multiple trajectory prediction models, ranging from physics-based to interaction-aware
learning architectures, are trained on the constructed trajectory windows to generate future
motion predictions.
Quantitative Evaluation:
● Model performance is systematically evaluated using standard prediction accuracy metrics,
namely Average Displacement Error (ADE) and Final Displacement Error (FDE), enabling
objective comparison across approaches.

Conclusion from Phase 2 Review 1

●

Worst Performance: MLP

●

Best ADE: MFP (Oracle) {Best at trajectory shape & uncertainty coverage (low ADE)}

●

Best FDE: Trajectron++* {Best at final goal selection (low FDE)}

●

Best Balanced Realistic Model: MFP(Learned)

* : The ADE and FDE error values obtained for Trajectron++ could potentially be further optimized. However, due to time
constraints, exhaustive hyperparameter tuning and extended training were not feasible, and therefore the reported
results do not represent the model’s fully optimized performance.

Suggestions from Phase 2 Review 1
The panel recommended improving model performance on rare but critical collision scenarios,
which are underrepresented in real-world datasets.
To address this, we are exploring synthetic collision datasets to augment training data
Planned approach:
● Use synthetic data to augment collision and near-collision cases
● Improve model’s ability to predict risky trajectories and edge-case behaviors
Goal:
Increase robustness and safety of trajectory prediction by training on collision-rich synthetic
scenarios that are difficult to capture in real-world data.

Conclusion from Phase 2 Review 2
RETRACTED (Jul 2026): a previous version of this slide reported a 66%/131% ADE/FDE
degradation for Trajectron++ on collision data. That figure was a placeholder not
backed by any experiment and has been retracted. The collision-split evaluation is
planned future work (see report Section 7.3 for the protocol).
● Any planner relying on a prediction model is only as safe as the predictions it receives; poor
collision-scenario forecasting directly limits collision avoidance capability.
● In the next phase, we aim to fine-tune Trajectron++ on synthetic collision data to close this
performance gap.
● We will evaluate whether improved predictions translate to measurable safety gains when
integrated into a framework.
● Once prediction quality is validated, we will identify an architecture best suited to unstructured,
collision-prone traffic conditions, this will serve as the foundation on which the remainder of the
project is built.
